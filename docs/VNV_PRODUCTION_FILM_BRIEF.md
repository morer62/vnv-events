# VNV Events production films

## Direction

Create three English-language films for the website, 40–45 seconds each in 16:9. Open on a problem or decision familiar to the buyer. Use real VNV footage and photographs for documented past work. Use photoreal Seedance shots for illustrative moments and cutaways. A viewer should be able to tell when they are seeing a real case study from the event caption.

Use a naturally paced human voice recording. Start with room sound or the first spoken line. Build transitions out of motion and sound. Cut from a hand reaching for a door to the welcome desk, from a stage light to a microphone, or from the movement of guests to a clean graphic. Leave the last two seconds for a readable title and one action. Add English captions and keep text inside the center-safe area for mobile.

## Questions for the script review

Act as a director and documentary copywriter for VNV Events. Refine the three scripts below for a spoken read of 40–45 seconds each. Make the buyer understand what VNV delivers in the first ten seconds. Keep the real Victoria case factual. Suggest one transition that connects the digital side of the work to a physical action on event day. Use plain American English, natural sentences and one action at the end of each film. Return a voiceover and a short shot-by-shot edit with sound cues. Flag any line that implies a result, client or service we have not documented.

## Seedance shot setup

Generate each illustrative shot separately at normal playback speed. Describe one camera move, one action and one location per shot. Use reference-free shots for people and hands. Keep the real Victoria portal, text, brand marks and documented photography as separate layers in CapCut. This gives the editor control over spelling, timing and which material represents actual past work.

Opening shot for Cause on Cue:

> Six seconds, 16:9. Documentary footage inside an elegant South Florida charity gala room just before guests arrive. The camera moves slowly through a doorway toward the welcome table, close to shoulder height. Natural daylight from tall windows, warm practical lighting, realistic linen and polished wood. A crew member crosses quietly out of focus. One continuous shot with natural motion and room ambience. Keep the frame free of legible words, logos and recognizable faces.
